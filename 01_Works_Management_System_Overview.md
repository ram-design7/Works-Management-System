# Works Management System

## 1. What is the Works Management System?

The Works Management System is a digital platform being built for the **SAI (Sports Authority of India) Engineering Division**. Its job is to manage the entire life of an infrastructure project — a new stadium, a running track, a hostel, an indoor hall, and so on — from the moment someone asks for it to the moment it is built, paid for, and recorded as a finished asset.

Think of it as covering every step of "we need a facility" → "we planned it" → "we approved it" → "we built it" → "we paid for it" → "we checked its quality" → "we officially closed the project and added it to our records."

Today, most of this is done manually using Excel sheets, paper registers, and physical files. There is no single system that shows the whole picture — information about ongoing projects, past works, vendors, contracts, milestones, payments, and quality checks is scattered across many files. The new system aims to bring all of this into one digital place.

## 2. Why is it needed? (Problems with the current manual process)

- No central, searchable list of SAI's existing buildings and infrastructure — so it's hard to know what already exists, when it was built, what it cost, or its current condition.
- No standard online form for centres to submit their infrastructure requirements; everything is consolidated manually in Excel.
- Proposals that need correction are sent back and forth with no clear audit trail of what changed and when.
- No system link between the approved list of works and the sheet that tracks which agency (CPWD, PSU, or in-house) is executing each work.
- Contracts, bank guarantees, and key dates (like the work start date) are tracked only on paper, making it hard to reference them quickly.
- Progress, payments, and quality-test records are kept in separate sheets and physical registers, so there is no live, unified view of a project's status.
- Once a project is finished, there is no automatic way to add it to a central asset register — so SAI's record of what it owns keeps falling out of date.

The redesigned (To-Be) system fixes each of these gaps by digitising the full process end to end.

## 3. Who is involved? (Stakeholders)

| Stakeholder | Role in the process |
|---|---|
| **Regional Centres (RCs), National Centres of Excellence (NCOEs), Sub-Training Centres (STCs), and States** | The "originating stakeholders" — they identify what infrastructure they need and raise proposals. STC/NCOE demands are grouped under their parent Regional Centre. |
| **SAI Engineering Division (Headquarters/HO)** | Owns and runs the whole works process — consolidates demands, prepares estimates, manages tendering, and monitors execution. This is the apex/HQ-level monitoring tier. |
| **Operations Division** | Takes part in reviewing and prioritising proposals along with the Regional Centre and Engineering Division. |
| **Director General (DG)** | The "competent authority" who gives in-principle approval, decides the execution route, and approves Administrative Approval/Technical Sanction on file. |
| **CPWD (Central Public Works Department)** | One of three possible executing agencies. Works are given to CPWD directly based on its fixed regional jurisdiction (no tender needed to select CPWD itself). |
| **PSU (Public Sector Undertaking, e.g., NPCC, NBCC)** | Another possible executing agency, selected through an open tender (lowest bidder, "L1"). |
| **In-house execution team (Engineering Division)** | The third route — SAI's own team directly hires a consultant/PMC and a contractor via separate tenders. |
| **Consultant / PMC (Project Management Consultant)** | Prepares detailed estimates and project reports (for in-house works) and supports SAI during execution. |
| **Contractor** | Physically carries out construction work (for in-house and, indirectly, for CPWD/PSU works via their own sub-tenders). |
| **Engineering In-charge** | The on-site engineer who supervises daily work, verifies contractor claims, signs completion certificates, and conducts site visits (with geo-tagged photos in the To-Be system). |
| **SAI Engineer (verification)** | Additionally verifies work quality/progress alongside the engineering in-charge. |
| **Accounts/Finance function** | Releases payments to contractors, PSUs, or CPWD once bills are verified and approved. |
| **Regional Centre (RC) monitoring tier** | Supervises projects within its jurisdiction — the "middle" level of oversight. |
| **Headquarters (HO) monitoring tier** | Provides consolidated, drill-down visibility across all centres and all projects — the "top" level of oversight. |

## 4. The Project Categories

Every proposal falls into one of these types of work:

- **Field of Play (FOP) Works** — running tracks, football turf, hockey turf, and similar outdoor playing surfaces.
- **Multipurpose Halls** — indoor halls for boxing and other indoor sports.
- **Hostels** — residential buildings for athletes, coaches, and support staff.
- **Residential and Administrative Offices**
- **Services** — supporting works like sewage treatment plants, drainage, internal roads, etc.

## 5. The Three Execution Routes

Once a project is approved in principle, it must be assigned one of three routes for how it will actually get built:

1. **CPWD Route** — The Central Public Works Department is directly given the work (based on its fixed jurisdiction, no tender for CPWD itself). CPWD then runs its own tender to hire a contractor.
2. **PSU Route** — A Public Sector Undertaking (like NPCC or NBCC) is selected through an open tender. The chosen PSU then hires its own contractor.
3. **In-house Route** — SAI's Engineering Division manages everything itself: it hires a consultant/PMC and a contractor through separate open tenders.

The Director General decides the route, usually by grouping similar categories of work together (e.g., all field-of-play works) rather than deciding route-by-route for each individual project.

## 6. The End-to-End Workflow (12 Stages)

Below is the complete journey of a project, shown as the improved ("To-Be") digital process. Each stage mirrors a step described in F.2.2, building on the manual As-Is process described in F.2.1.

### Stage 1 — Proposal Creation
The originating stakeholder (STC, RC, NCOE, State, or HO) logs into the system and creates a new proposal, filling in: category of work, work type, name of the work, brief description, scope of work, and estimated cost. If the work is an upgrade to an existing asset, it can be linked directly to that asset's record. Proposals can be saved as drafts and submitted when ready. Every proposal's status is visible in real time, and users can track all their proposals in one view.

### Stage 2 — Proposal Review & In-Principle Approval
The proposal is routed to relevant divisions for comments, then jointly reviewed and prioritised by the Operations Division, the Regional Centre, and the Engineering Division. The Engineering Division prepares a prioritised, centre-wise list of works for the year, which goes to the Director General for in-principle approval (based on the rough/indicative cost, not a detailed estimate). Once approved, centres are notified of what's approved and at what cost.

### Stage 3 — Execution Route Decision
The Director General decides how each approved work (or category of works) will be executed: CPWD, PSU, or in-house. This decision, and the agency assigned, is recorded against the work and carried forward automatically from the approval stage.

### Stage 4 — Estimation, Administrative Approval & Technical Sanction
A detailed cost estimate is now prepared (by CPWD, the PSU, or the in-house consultant, depending on the route), based on the applicable Schedule of Rates. The Engineering Division reviews it and may request revisions. Once finalised, the work receives **Administrative Approval** (formal sign-off to proceed at the estimated cost) and then **Technical Sanction**, at which point a detailed Bill of Quantities (BOQ) — listing every work item, quantity, and rate — is prepared and readied for tendering.

### Stage 5 — Tendering
Depending on the route:
- **CPWD route**: CPWD tenders for its own contractor.
- **PSU route**: SAI floats an open tender to pick the PSU; the PSU then tenders for its contractor.
- **In-house route**: SAI floats separate open tenders for a consultant/PMC and for a contractor.

Every individual work gets its own dedicated tender. All key tender details (dates, costs, budget head, etc.) are tracked centrally, with live status visibility.

### Stage 6 — Contract Award
Once the lowest bidder (L1) is determined:
1. A **Letter of Acceptance (LOA)** is issued.
2. The contractor submits a **Performance Bank Guarantee (PBG)** as security.
3. The **Contract Agreement** is signed, covering scope, payment milestones, penalty clauses, defect liability period, and warranty terms.
4. A **Letter of Commencement (LOC)** is issued, officially starting the work and the countdown for all project timelines.

### Stage 7 — Execution & Field Monitoring
Work begins on the ground. Progress is updated regularly (component/line-item level) on the portal. An **Engineering In-charge** is assigned to each project to record progress and conduct site visits — with **geo-tagged photos** in the new digital system for verifiable proof of visits. Both physical progress (planned vs. achieved) and financial progress (funds released) are tracked together. Monitoring happens at both the Regional Centre level and Headquarters level, with automatic weekly/periodic reports generated from live data (instead of manually compiled reports).

### Stage 8 — Measurement, Claim & Payment
As work is completed, quantities are measured and logged in the **Measurement Book** against the BOQ. The contractor raises a claim/invoice for completed work, which the Engineering In-charge verifies on-site before it is approved internally and sent for payment. Any penalties for delay or poor quality are deducted at this stage. Payments then flow through SAI's own accounts (in-house route) or via funds released to CPWD/PSU (their routes), following agreed milestones.

### Stage 9 — Quality Assurance & Testing
Three types of testing ensure the work meets standards:
- **Field testing** (in-house, by SAI-authorised staff)
- **Material testing** (in-house, by SAI-authorised staff)
- **Third-party audit testing** (done by the contractor per the contract, with full reports shared)

All test records are now captured digitally against the work, instead of only in physical registers.

### Stage 10 — Project Closure
When work is physically complete, it is handed over. The final bill is prepared based on closing measurements. Closure documents (drawings, test reports, etc.) are collected progressively throughout the project so they're ready at handover. On satisfactory verification, the Engineering In-charge issues a **Completion Certificate**. At this point the work is "physically complete" but not yet financially closed.

### Stage 11 — Post-Closure: Defect Liability, Financial Settlement & Asset Records
After handover, the work enters the **Defect Liability Period (DLP)** — typically 1–2 years (with some items like synthetic tracks carrying warranties of 7–10 years) — during which the contractor must fix any defects that appear. The system tracks DLP and warranty deadlines with alerts. Once the DLP is satisfactorily completed, the final bill is settled, the balance payment released, and any retention/security money returned — marking the work as **fully and financially closed**.

### Stage 12 — Asset Record Creation (automatic, tied into Stage 11 and the wider ecosystem)
On full closure, the completed work **automatically flows into the National Facility Register / asset inventory** as an operational facility — capturing details like year of construction, cost, and area — instead of relying on manual, inconsistent record-keeping as happens today. From then on, any future upgradation or maintenance work on that asset is recorded against this same asset record, building a full lifecycle history over time.

## 7. Key Documents & Terms Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **In-Principle Approval** | Initial go-ahead from the Director General based on a rough/indicative cost, before detailed estimation begins. |
| **Administrative Approval** | Formal approval to carry out a work at its detailed estimated cost. |
| **Technical Sanction** | Approval of the detailed Bill of Quantities (BOQ), forming the basis for tendering. |
| **BOQ (Bill of Quantities)** | Item-by-item list of work with quantities and rates, used for tendering and billing. |
| **DPR (Detailed Project Report)** | The full technical/financial report prepared for in-house works, following a master plan and Preliminary Project Report (PPR). |
| **LOA (Letter of Acceptance)** | Formal notice awarding the contract to the winning bidder. |
| **PBG (Performance Bank Guarantee)** | Security deposit from the contractor guaranteeing performance. |
| **LOC (Letter of Commencement)** | Document fixing the official start date of work; all project timelines are counted from this date. |
| **Measurement Book (MB)** | Record of actual quantities of work executed, used to value and verify contractor claims. |
| **DLP (Defect Liability Period)** | Post-completion period (usually 1–2 years) during which the contractor must fix defects free of charge. |
| **L1** | Industry shorthand for the lowest bidder in a tender, who is typically awarded the contract. |
| **DSR (Delhi Schedule of Rates)** | The standard rate list used to prepare cost estimates. |

## 8. What Changes from As-Is to To-Be (Summary of Improvements)

- **Single digital platform** replacing scattered Excel sheets and paper registers for the entire proposal-to-closure lifecycle.
- **Standardised online proposal form** (with configurable categories) instead of ad-hoc submissions.
- **Real-time status visibility** for every proposal and project, for both the originating centre and monitoring levels (RC and HQ).
- **Automatic linking** between approval stages — e.g., approved work details carry forward automatically into route assignment and estimation, removing manual re-entry and disconnected tracking sheets.
- **Digitally captured contracts** — LOA, PBG, agreement terms, and commencement dates are all stored against the work record instead of only in physical files.
- **Geo-tagged site visits** and digitally recorded progress/quality data instead of physical registers.
- **Automatic asset registration** — completed projects flow straight into the central asset/facility register, keeping SAI's infrastructure records always up to date.
- **Built-in alerts** for Defect Liability Period and warranty deadlines, so nothing is missed after project handover.
