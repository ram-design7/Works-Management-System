# Works Management System — Personas


This document translates the stakeholders of the Works Management System into six realistic, individual personas — each grounded directly in the roles described in the BRD, with their own background, day-to-day frustrations, and definition of success. These personas are the foundation for the Jobs-to-be-Done analysis in the companion document.

Each persona follows the same structure:

- **Profile** — who they are and where they sit in the process.
- **Current Pains (As-Is)** — what makes their work harder today, drawn directly from the As-Is gaps in the BRD.
- **Where they touch the workflow** — which of the 12 workflow stages they interact with.
- **Success looks like** — a measurable, human signal that things are working well for them.

---

## Persona 1: Priya — the Centre-Level Proposal Originator

**Profile:** Works at a Regional Centre / STC / NCOE (or a State Sports Department) as the point person who identifies infrastructure needs — a damaged track, an overdue hostel upgrade, a new multipurpose hall — and puts them forward to SAI Headquarters. She is not primarily an engineer; she is a coordinator balancing athlete and centre needs against what she knows will realistically get funded.

**Current Pains (As-Is):**
- No standard template — every centre submits demands differently, so proposals get bounced back for missing details.
- Cost estimates are often missing at submission and get patched in later at HQ, weakening her ownership of the number.
- No visibility once a proposal leaves her hands — no way to check status without phoning HQ.
- Corrections mean a full round-trip of re-submission with no record of what changed.

**Where she touches the workflow:** Stage 1 (Proposal Creation) primarily; re-enters at Stage 2 if the proposal is returned for revision; passively monitors Stages 3–12 as her project moves through the pipeline.

**Success looks like:** She can tell an athlete or coach exactly which stage their facility request is at, on demand, without making a single phone call to Delhi.

---

## Persona 2: Rakesh — the HQ Engineering Division Desk Officer (Reviewer/Estimator)

**Profile:** Sits within the SAI Engineering Division at Headquarters. Consolidates demands from all centres, coordinates with the Operations Division, tracks estimates coming back from CPWD/PSU/consultants, and prepares the prioritised list that goes up to the Director General.

**Current Pains (As-Is):**
- Consolidation is manual, in Excel, with no standard intake — errors and omissions creep in.
- No system link between the in-principle-approved list and the route-assignment sheet — he re-keys data.
- Estimate revisions, observation dates, and agency responses are tracked in yet another disconnected sheet.
- No audit trail of how or why a demand was modified or reprioritised.

**Where he touches the workflow:** Stages 2 through 6 most intensively (Review & In-Principle Approval, Route Decision, Estimation/Sanction, Tendering, Contract Award); retains oversight visibility through Stages 7–12.

**Success looks like:** The prioritised, centre-wise list for DG approval is generated from live system data in minutes, not compiled overnight before the meeting.

---

## Persona 3: The Director General (DG) — the Competent Authority

**Profile:** The apex decision-maker who grants in-principle approval, decides execution routes, and signs off on Administrative Approval and Technical Sanction. Oversees the entire national portfolio of works across all centres, categories, and routes — needs the big picture, not line-item detail, unless something is off-track.

**Current Pains (As-Is):**
- Decisions are recorded only in file notings (via e-Office), disconnected from the underlying data sheets — hard to trace which approval applies to which live work.
- No consolidated, drill-down view across all centres, categories, and agencies; portfolio health has to be pieced together from separate reports.
- Route decisions are grouped by category with no structured record of the rationale, making later review or audit difficult.

**Where he/she touches the workflow:** Stage 2 (In-Principle Approval), Stage 3 (Route Decision), Stage 4 (Administrative Approval/Technical Sanction) as direct decision gates; retains portfolio-level oversight across all 12 stages.

**Success looks like:** Any work's full approval history and current status can be pulled up in seconds during a review meeting, with no need to request files in advance.

---

## Persona 4: The Engineering In-charge — the Site-Level Executor

**Profile:** A SAI engineer physically present at (or closely responsible for) the work site. Supervises daily execution, verifies contractor claims against actual work done, conducts site visits, and issues the completion certificate at handover. Represents both the Regional Centre and Headquarters monitoring tiers on the ground.

**Current Pains (As-Is):**
- Contract terms, PBG details, and commencement dates aren't systematically linked to the work — he may not have quick access to the governing terms during execution.
- Progress and quality-test records live in physical registers at the site, vulnerable to loss and hard to consolidate for reporting.
- No structured, verifiable record of site visits beyond written notes.

**Where he touches the workflow:** Central to Stage 7 (Execution & Field Monitoring), Stage 8 (Measurement, Claim & Payment), Stage 9 (Quality Assurance & Testing), Stage 10 (Project Closure), and Stage 11 (Post-Closure/DLP).

**Success looks like:** Every claim he certifies is backed by geo-tagged, timestamped evidence that would hold up if questioned in an audit — with zero reliance on a physical register that could be misplaced.

---

## Persona 5: The Executing Agency Representative — CPWD Officer / PSU Project Manager / Private Contractor

**Profile:** An external partner delivering the physical work — could be a CPWD divisional officer, a PSU (e.g., NPCC/NBCC) project manager, or a private contractor engaged under the in-house route. Their success is measured by getting paid accurately and on time for verified work, and by avoiding disputes or penalty deductions they consider unfair.

**Current Pains (As-Is):**
- Payment verification depends entirely on manual site sign-off, with no shared, real-time view of what's been measured and certified.
- Penalty deductions (for delay or workmanship) are applied without an easily referenceable, transparent record tied to specific contract clauses.
- For CPWD/PSU routes, fund-release timing (advance vs. staged, per the applicable works manual) is not always visible in advance, complicating cash-flow planning.

**Where they touch the workflow:** Stage 5 (Tendering, as bidder), Stage 6 (Contract Award), Stage 7 (Execution), Stage 8 (Measurement, Claim & Payment), Stage 9 (Quality Testing — for third-party audit testing), Stage 11 (DLP obligations).

**Success looks like:** Claims are verified and paid within a predictable, known timeframe, with clear visibility into any deductions and why they were applied.

---

## Persona 6: The Headquarters Portfolio Monitor — RC/HQ Oversight Officer

**Profile:** Sits at the Regional Centre or Headquarters monitoring tier (distinct from the DG) — responsible for tracking the health of all works within a jurisdiction (RC level) or across the country (HO level), compiling the weekly/fortnightly reports, and escalating at-risk projects.

**Current Pains (As-Is):**
- Progress is tracked via a manually updated monitoring sheet with varying formats across centres (weekly and fortnightly reports each follow their own format).
- No drill-down from a consolidated national view into an individual project without requesting the underlying files.
- Report compilation itself consumes time that could go into actually managing at-risk works.

**Where they touch the workflow:** Continuous oversight across Stages 7–12, with particular emphasis on Stage 7 (Execution & Field Monitoring).

**Success looks like:** At-risk works are identified from the dashboard days or weeks before they would have surfaced in a manually compiled report.

## Personas at a Glance

| Persona | Base Location | Primary Role in Process | Workflow Stages Touched |
|---|---|---|---|
| Priya — Centre Proposal Originator | RC / STC / NCOE / State | Raises and tracks infrastructure demand | 1–2 (primary), 3–12 (monitors) |
| Rakesh — HQ Desk Officer/Estimator | SAI HQ, Engineering Division | Consolidates, reviews, and routes proposals | 2–6 (primary), 7–12 (oversight) |
| Director General — Competent Authority | SAI HQ | Approves at every major decision gate | 2, 3, 4 (gates), all stages (oversight) |
| Engineering In-charge — Site Executor | Work site / Regional Centre | Supervises, verifies, and certifies execution | 7, 8, 9, 10, 11 |
| Executing Agency Rep — CPWD/PSU/Contractor | External agency | Delivers the physical work and raises claims | 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 11 |
| HQ/RC Portfolio Monitor | Regional Centre / SAI HQ | Tracks portfolio health and escalates risk | 7–12 |
